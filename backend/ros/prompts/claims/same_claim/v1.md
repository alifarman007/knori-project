Two statements about the same person. Decide one thing: **are they the same claim?**

Same claim means a person reading both would say KnoRI is telling them the same thing
twice. Different wording is fine. Different meaning is not.

This answer is used to block a claim the person has already rejected, to avoid showing them
the same card twice, and to avoid announcing something they already told KnoRI outright.
Saying "same" wrongly hides something true; saying "different" wrongly repeats something
they have already dealt with. Be strict, and when it is genuinely borderline, answer
`false`.

## What makes them different

- **Negation.** "They avoid conflict" and "They do not avoid conflict" are opposites, not
  the same claim. Never answer `true` when one negates the other.
- **Scope.** "They avoid conflict" and "They avoid conflict at work" are different: one is
  narrower. Same for time ("used to") and for people ("with family").
- **Direction.** "They decide, then discuss" and "They discuss, then decide" describe
  opposite orders.
- **Strength.** "They sometimes put things off" and "They put everything off" are
  different claims about how much.
- **Subject.** Anything about a different person is a different claim.

## What does not make them different

- Wording, tense, tone, or one being longer than the other.
- One naming a topic the other implies, when the substance is identical.
- One being written for the person to read and the other written internally.

## Examples

| A | B | same |
|---|---|---|
| They work best early in the morning | They get their best work done before the day starts | true |
| They put off invoicing | They put off admin tasks | false — one is narrower |
| They avoid difficult conversations | They prepare before difficult conversations rather than avoiding them | false — it contradicts A |
| They start more projects than they finish | They rarely finish what they start | true |
| They are vegetarian | They stopped eating meat two years ago | true |
| They feel drained after big groups | They dislike parties | false — one is about after, the other about wanting to go |

## Answer

- `same`: true or false.
- `why`: one sentence. If false, name the difference — negation, scope, direction,
  strength or subject.
