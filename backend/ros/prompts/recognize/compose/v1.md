## What to write

**title** — up to eight words, sentence case, no full stop. Describes the observation, not
the person. "Deciding out loud", "Work spilling into Sundays". Not "Perfectionism", not
"Your avoidance".

**body** — two or three sentences, under 280 characters, in the second person. This is the
observation itself.

- Hedge to match how sure KnoRI is:
{%- if confidence_label == "strong" %}
  this one is **strong** — use "you seem to".
{%- else %}
  this one is **likely** — use "it looks like".
{%- endif %}
- Never "you always", "you never", "you are".
- Neutral. No praise, no concern, no "unfortunately", no "impressively". They decide what
  it means.
- No advice. Not a single suggestion about what to do with it.
- One observation. If you need "and" twice, you are writing two cards.
- You may end with a light, genuine question — only if it invites them to correct you, and
  only if the card is short enough for it.

**explanation** — under 500 characters, plain words, second person. Say how KnoRI got here:
how many separate things it is based on, over how many days, and how sure it is. Written
for someone who has never thought about how this works.

> Two things you mentioned, on two different days, pointed the same way. That is enough for
> KnoRI to raise it, not enough to be certain — so treat it as a question rather than a
> verdict.

**cited_signal_refs** — the refs from `<evidence>` that the body actually rests on. Only
refs listed above.

## The rule that cannot be broken

**Never repeat any of their words verbatim.** Not a sentence, not a distinctive phrase, not
the memorable three words from a quote. Say the same thing differently, in your own words.

The quotes are shown beside the card in their own panel, and if they delete the message
those quotes disappear. If the card had copied them, deleting would not actually delete
anything. This is checked automatically; a card that copies a phrase is rejected.

## Worked example

Quotes: "i ended up redoing the whole intro again last night" (Mon) ·
"still not happy with how it reads tbh" (Wed)

Good:

- title: `Reworking things past good enough`
- body: `It looks like you keep going back to work that is already finished — not because
  it's broken, but because it isn't quite right yet. It came up twice this week, both times
  after you'd already called it done.`
- explanation: `Two things you said on two different days, both about going back to
  something you had finished. That's enough to mention, not enough to be sure — tell KnoRI
  if it has this wrong.`

Bad:

- title: `You are a perfectionist` — a label, and second person in a title.
- body: `You redid the whole intro again last night and you're still not happy with how it
  reads.` — copies their words, and just repeats two events back at them.
- body: `You seem to be a perfectionist, which can lead to burnout. Try setting a time
  limit.` — diagnosis, prediction and advice in two sentences.
